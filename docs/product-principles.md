# Life OS — Product Principles

## Keeper of the Hearth test

Every meaningful product decision should survive two questions:

1. Does this notice the user without trying to define them?
2. Does this still feel like home?

## Core principles

1. **No streaks.** Missing days is information, not failure.
2. **No guilt.** The app never punishes disengagement.
3. **Everything is optional.** Even reflection.
4. **Truth over consistency.** One honest check-in is more valuable than seven performed ones.
5. **Earn depth.** Deep questions require trust, context, and appropriate timing.
6. **Comfort is a valid need.** Not every difficult moment should be converted into growth work.
7. **The human is the authority.** Patterns are offered as invitations and can be corrected or rejected.
8. **The app may become quieter.** Success can mean needing less guidance over time.
9. **Meaning is not positivity.** Grief, anger, uncertainty, and loss can produce some of the brightest stars.
10. **Technology stays backstage.** AI and mathematics serve the experience rather than becoming the experience.
11. **Warmth is not sanitized language.** The product may learn a user's humor, irreverence, softness, profanity, metaphors, and preferred emotional register — with context-sensitive restraint.
12. **Easy to use is a product requirement.** The user should not need documentation to experience the Hearth.

## Product findings

### Finding #001

> Keeper of the Hearth does not wish to fuck with ZIP files on her phone.

**Interpretation:** Delivery is part of UX. The product must be tap → works → feels good.

**Severity:** Critical.

### Finding #002

Warmth does not require sanitized wellness-app language. Voice should adapt to the person's authentic register while remaining sensitive to context.

### Finding #003

If the user needs documentation to use Life OS, we have fucked up.

### Finding #004

Knowing JavaScript does not mean the Keeper of the Hearth should become the deployment engineer. Technical complexity belongs behind the walls.
